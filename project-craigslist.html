Chapter 01

Craigslist is broken.

Craigslist has looked the same since 1995. It's a wall of text, with no images, no discovery, and no hierarchy: just a wall of links.

The landing page is cluttered, hard to read and parse, and completely eschews accessible design practices. All links are rendered as blue plaintext, and there are minimal icons and headers to draw attention to features.

Chapter 02

Heuristic Evaluation.

We wanted to make Craiglist better, so we performed a comprehensive heuristic evaluation in order to figure out how best to improve. What concerns did we identify?

  • Dense, cluttered text
  • Fragile search filters
  • Inconsistent navigation structure
  • Excessive white space
Two pages from our heuristic report.
03
Chapter 03

Low Fidelity Design Concepts.

After completing our heuristic critique, we put together several concepts for our redesigned site, focusing on functionality and aesthetics. Our main goals were to increase readability, implement a visual hierarchy, and totally redesign navigation.

Read our full design concept below:

We are redesigning Craigslist, a '90s era messageboard and swap/sell site. Craigslist suffers from several design problems centered around navigation, readability, and text density. Firstly, users fail to parse the homepage because of the quantity and size of text (every category on the site is listed on the homepage). We will improve this by collapsing departments and categories into a hamburger menu and funneling users to find postings through search, rather than navigating the site's category tree. Secondly, users struggle to find and use the search feature, because it occupies little space on the homepage compared to the rest of the site's text. We will resolve this by making the search bar larger, more centrally located, and by changing its iconography to be more recognizable. Thirdly, users experience eye strain because of the current text sizing, color, and whitespace on the site– the homepage is stuck in a roughly 4:3 aspect ratio with huge white bezels, all clickable text is rendered as plain blue links, and all text is at a roughly 12pt font. We will improve the site's aesthetic by refactoring the site's visual hierarchy, making more intentional font and color choices, and making better use of the site's empty space. Finally, we will also improve Craigslist by including the required interaction features, including a responsive, mobile-friendly interface, visible search/load indicators, feedback forms, and a dark mode (other accessibility improvements are covered by our text changes).

One of six homepage prototypes. This is the design we ultimately chose.

Chapter 04

Prototype Usability Testing.

Before commiting to our prototype design, we undertook extensive user testing to ensure our hierarchy and flow were legible and functional. We created mock sketches of the site for users to pretend to interact with.

We had users attempt the following tasks:

  • Navigate the home page
  • Open the hamburger menu
  • Modify a setting on your account page
  • Use the search page to search for an item
  • Use the filter
  • Choose an item and open its listing
  • Use the social feed and make a post

Our results were clear: the home page, listing pages, and hamburher menu were clear, but we needed to refine our search and social approaches.

Page one of our annotated and analyzed testing feedback.

Chapter 05

Wireframing.

After refining our intiial sketches and user testing we created six low fidelity wireframes in Figma for further testing. We focused on key pages, interaction states, and user flows.

Crucially, our were mobile first, and then adapted to desktop use. By designing both at once, we avoided dramatic retooling later on.

Our low fidelity home screen redesign in Figma.
06
Chapter 06

Wireframe Testing.

After designing our wireframe, we conducted further user testing in order to ensure functionality.

We asked users to complete the following tasks, and track their navigation paths as they did so.

  • Find a product using the search feature and open the product page
  • Navigate to a seller's profile and view their ratings
  • Return to the homepage
  • Open the hamburger menu and access your account

The results of our testing indicated that all of our user flows were successful except for navigating to a seller's profile and viewing their ratings. User comments indiciated that finding the page and identifying the seller rating on the page was difficult.

One page from our wireframe test result indicating issues with the seller page.

Chapter 08

User Testing the Prototype.

We continued our rigorous testing by ensuring our interactive prototype was more navigable than our wireframe and asking users to complete tasks whose interfaces had undergone redesign.

Our testing showed that our final design would need more continous back-navigation, notification trays, and dark mode system status settings. We also concluded our visual hierarchy could be strong on seller and user pages.

09
Chapter 09

Responsive Implementation.

We built the redesign mobile-first, then scaled up — reversing the approach that produced Craigslist's current mobile experience. Every breakpoint was tested on real devices, not just browser resize handles.

The homepage, listing page, and search results each have dedicated responsive behaviors. Navigation collapses into a persistent bottom bar on small screens. Filters appear as a bottom sheet. Tap targets meet the 44px minimum throughout.

Our redesigned Craigslist homepage.
10
Chapter 10

Search.

Search was redesigned as the primary entry point rather than a secondary feature. A persistent search bar sits at the top of every screen, with contextual filters that adapt to the active category — showing only what's relevant.

The results page introduces a split view: a scannable list on the right and a live map on the left. Filters are sticky and remember their state between sessions. Saved searches can be named and revisited from a user's profile.

Chapter 11

Animations.

We designed a motion system to reinforce hierarchy and reduce cognitive load during page transitions. Listings slide up as they load, filters animate in from the side, and the map pans smoothly between result sets.

All animations stay under 300ms and respect the user's reduced-motion preference. The goal was for motion to feel like a natural response to interaction — not a performance.

12
Chapter 12

Sign-up and Validation.

We redesigned the sign-up flow from scratch, reducing it from nine steps to three. Each field validates inline as the user types, with clear error states and recovery suggestions — no more submitting a full form only to find out one field was wrong.

Email verification and phone confirmation were consolidated into a single step. For returning users, we introduced persistent sessions with biometric login support on mobile.

The result was a measurable drop in sign-up abandonment during testing, with users consistently completing registration without needing to restart.

Sign-up flow.
13
Chapter 13

Accessibility and Dark Mode.

Every screen was audited against WCAG 2.1 AA. We corrected contrast ratios across the type scale, added visible focus states to all interactive elements, and ensured the full interface is navigable by keyboard alone.

Dark mode was built as a true parallel theme — not an inverted version of the light one. Each color was re-evaluated for its dark context, with adjusted contrast and saturation to avoid eye strain on OLED displays.

Dark mode interface.
14
Chapter 14

Takeaways.

This project reinforced that the hardest redesigns aren't the ones where everything is broken. They're the ones where something works — and you have to be disciplined enough not to break it while you fix everything around it.

We came away with a stronger instinct for when to preserve and when to replace, a cleaner process for translating research findings into design decisions, and a full handoff-ready system we're proud to put our name on.
